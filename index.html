Method

Readable travel signals, not official risk scoring

The gradients are practical planning layers based on recent travel guides, nightlife reporting, neighborhood descriptions, and common visitor advice. They are intentionally fuzzy: a single building, hotel, or street can feel different from the wider area.

The default view is social-first: beauty/style heat, universities, student accommodation, and student hangout pins. Click More info to reveal the accommodation and sleep-planning tools.

"Visible street hardship" means places where visitors more often report rougher public-space conditions, shelters, station underpasses, or people asking for help. It is not a judgement of people experiencing homelessness, and it is not a safety accusation.

Click-to-score uses Google Places for nearby bars, nightclubs, and transit stations, then combines that with local noise/junction heuristics. Google Maps does not directly expose traffic-light noise, so corner and high-street risk is inferred from busy junctions, station edges, and whether the exact clicked point sits very close to a major road corridor. Central side streets are not treated as noisy just because they are central. Ordinary bars are a mild signal; nightclubs and club-like venues are weighted much more heavily because bass, queues, taxis, and late spillover are the real sleep risk.

  • Pick green areas for a first visit or sleep-sensitive trip.
  • Use purple/red noise layers to avoid party streets and train-station edges.
  • Use gold/blue layers when you want polished hotels, views, and calmer streets.
